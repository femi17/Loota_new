The Bronze Compass: how it's solved

Five keys, all hidden in the story. None of the five places is ever named in it. Players have to dig each one out, and every key has false trails built in. Three rules run through the whole story; spotting them is what separates careful players from everyone else.

🐆 The true sealEhizogie's letters carry a leopard whose tail curls to the left (prologue). The Masquerade's forgeries curl to the right: the forged strip in Lisbon and the doctored radio notice.
📻 Six, never sevenGrandpa's note says the six o'clock news. The soft-voiced driver says "the seven o'clock, it's done": the Masquerade changed the seven o'clock bulletin.
🔔 Count, then walk backwardsGrandpa's church game (chapter 1) is the key to the Lisbon code: the number of bells is how many letters to step back through the alphabet.
22 decoys

🗝️ 1 Where did Grandpa tell Isoken to begin?

Igun Street, Benin City (the bronze casters' street)
  1. The story never says what the newsreader said between the weather and the football. The bridge and the driver cut it out (chapter 2).
  2. Players open the radio in the game and tune to Radio Benin 97.3, a station that exists only during this hunt. It plays the six o'clock and seven o'clock bulletins in turn.
  3. In the six o'clock news, the only notice between the weather and the football is the brass leopard (tail curled left) handed in at the casters' guild on Igun Street.

Decoys: the seven o'clock bulletin (its notice says the leopard, tail curled right, is at the National Museum on King's Square, where a trap chest waits); about 18 other notices across both bulletins, each naming a real Benin City street or place (Sakponba Road, Akpakpava Road, Lagos Street, Ihama Road, Airport Road, Oba Market, Siluko Road, Plymouth Road, Ikpokpan Road, Ekenhuan Road, Second West Circular Road, Benin-Sapele Road, Mission Road…); plus the places from Grandpa's life in chapters 1 and 3 (his sister off Siluko Road, corn on Oba Market Road, traffic on Akpakpava Road, the chop bar on Mission Road).

Why AI can't help: the answer isn't in the text at all. It's in a broadcast you have to hear, at the right hour.

13 decoys

🗝️ 2 Where did Isoken fly?

Belém, Lisbon (the riverside where the old stones are: Belém Tower)
  1. Pa Eweka plays five voicemails. Four are to Eweka; only the fifth ends "This one is for Isoken".
  2. The first letters of its sentences spell the place: Bring, Every, Listen, Eat, Mind, which gives BELÉM.
  3. Chapter 4 confirms it without naming it: a river wide as the sea, sardines, old yellow trams, a giant statue with open arms across the water, a monastery's bells, old stones by the water.

Decoys: the other four voicemails also spell places: PORTO, ROMA, ACCRA, LAGOS (Grandpa's false trails for the Masquerade's listeners). The masked man's luggage tags: FCO (Rome), IST (Istanbul), EBB (Entebbe, for Kampala). "The city of the seven hills": Rome, Istanbul, Kampala, Yaoundé are all mentioned. In chapter 4: the open-armed statue looks like Rio de Janeiro's, and in Lisbon itself the monastery (Jerónimos), the statue on the far bank (Cristo Rei) and the Monument to the Discoveries are near-misses.

Possible additions to reach 20: more of Pa Eweka's memories of Grandpa's travels, and a second false tower in chapter 4.

🗝️ 3 Which city and street did Isoken work out by dawn?

Great Russell Street, London (the British Museum)
  1. Two brass strips in Lisbon. The one with the left-curled leopard is real: RCPLE CFDDPWW.
  2. She counted eleven bells. Grandpa's game: that many steps, then walk backwards.
  3. Shift every letter 11 places back through the alphabet: GREAT RUSSELL.

Decoys: the forged strip (right-curled leopard) XLI LSVRMQER decodes with 4 steps to THE HORNIMAN, a real south London museum that returned 72 Benin objects; its pencil note "Meet me at the Tower. M" points to the Tower of London. The news article in chapter 4 adds a Cambridge college (the bronze cockerel), a small south London museum, a university in Aberdeen, and a city in Scotland. Wrong shift counts give nonsense and waste time.

Needs more: the next draft adds more museums and cities that hold bronzes (for example Oxford, Bristol and Liverpool), woven into the hostel chapter.

🗝️ 4 Where was the Masquerade when he called?

Humboldt Forum, Berlin (the rebuilt palace)
  1. He calls at 9:00 in London on a November morning and says "here it is already ten": he's one hour ahead, in Central European time. That rules out London and Lisbon.
  2. His clues: a king's house "built twice", "no king has slept for a century", cathedral bells "next door", "looking across the gardens at the museums".
  3. Margaret described nine palaces in that time zone over a long chat. Only Berlin's matches every one (pulled down after the war, rebuilt a few years ago, next to the cathedral, across the gardens from the museums).

Decoys (all real, all one hour ahead of London in November): Vienna (the emperors' palace full of the world's treasures), Copenhagen (palace on its island, built three times), Stockholm (palace next to the old church, museum across the water, but kings slept there within a century), Madrid (palace and cathedral side by side; a king lived there 95 years ago), Paris (the Louvre: a king's palace full of the world, but never rebuilt), Prague (a cathedral inside the castle walls), Budapest (palace on the hill above the river), Warsaw (castle rebuilt brick by brick, but no gardens of museums), and Berlin's own cathedral and museums next door.

Needs more: a few more Central European palaces in Margaret's memories, and one false "it's already eleven" clue for the wrong time zone.

6 decoys

🗝️ 5 Where did the compass lead Isoken home?

The Oba's Palace, Benin City
  1. The last letter repeats Ehizogie's words from the prologue: "Home is where the leopard sits on the king's wall."
  2. The king of Benin is the Oba; the bronze plaques once hung on his palace's walls and pillars (the prologue mentions "the plaques from the king's pillars").

Decoys: in the epilogue the compass leads her past the museum at the roundabout (full of leopards), Oba Market, the statue of Emotan the market woman, Igun Street and Pa Eweka's workshop. Earlier chapters add museums abroad that hold leopards.

Needs more: the final key is meant to be the gentlest, but more Benin City places with "king" in their name or story can be added (for example King's Square itself).

📻 Radio Benin 97.3: the two bulletins (to record in ElevenLabs)

The station appears in the game's radio only during this hunt and plays the two bulletins one after the other, each announced by its hour. The narrator's voice is dry, tidy and old-fashioned. The six o'clock is true; the seven o'clock was doctored by the Masquerade's people.

The six o'clock news (the real one)

[Pips.] Good evening. This is Radio Benin, ninety-seven point three. The news at six o'clock.

The state assembly has adjourned without agreement on the new bridge across the Ikpoba river. A passenger ferry was held up for three hours this morning by fog on the river. The price of petrol rose again at filling stations across the city. And the wedding of the year: guests travelled from four continents to celebrate the marriage of the highlife singer known as Lady Ivie.

Notices

A blue Peugeot left for repair at a mechanic's yard on Sakponba Road more than a year ago will be sold if not collected by the end of the month. The traders' association of Akpakpava Road meets on Saturday at ten. A goat found wandering on Lagos Street is being cared for by the residents of number fourteen, who would like it to stop eating their washing. Water supply will be interrupted on Ihama Road tomorrow between nine and two.

Weather

And now, the weather. A hot and humid evening across the city, with thunderstorms expected later over the eastern districts, clearing by morning.

Lost and found. A small brass leopard, sitting, its tail curled to the left, has been handed in at the bronze casters' guild on Igun Street. Its owner may collect it from the workshop with the painted leopard over the door, any morning, before the hammers stop.

Football

In football, a penalty in the eighty-ninth minute gave the home side a two-one victory in front of a full stadium…

Notices

The harvest service at the church on Airport Road is on Sunday at eight. A pair of reading glasses was found at Oba Market and may be collected from the market office. The bridge on Siluko Road will be closed for repairs next week. Road works continue on Plymouth Road. That is the news at six o'clock. Good evening.

The seven o'clock news (the doctored one)

[Pips.] Good evening. This is Radio Benin, ninety-seven point three. The news at seven o'clock.

[The same four headlines, word for word.]

Notices

Members of the Ikpokpan Road residents' association are reminded that their dues are now due. Lost: a black umbrella with a carved handle, left in a taxi near Ekenhuan Road. Free eye tests this weekend at the clinic on Second West Circular Road. Expect heavy traffic on the Benin-Sapele Road after the evening service.

Weather

[The same weather.]

Lost and found. A small brass leopard, sitting, its tail curled to the right, has been handed in at the National Museum on King's Square. Its owner may collect it at the front desk.

Football

[The same football.]

Notices

The chop bar on Mission Road known to its regulars as "Prof's Corner" will reopen on Monday. Choir practice at the church on Airport Road is cancelled this week. That is the news at seven o'clock. Good night.
